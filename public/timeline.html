🏛️ Australian History Timeline

From 1788 to Present - Essential Dates for Citizenship Test

1788

26 January - First Fleet Arrives

First Fleet from Great Britain arrives with convicts and settlers. Captain Arthur Phillip becomes the first Governor. This marks the beginning of European settlement in Australia. This date is now celebrated as Australia Day.

1851

Gold Discovered

Gold is discovered in the colonies of New South Wales and Victoria, triggering a major gold rush. This attracts thousands of immigrants from around the world and dramatically increases Australia's population.

1861

Responsible Government

Australian colonies begin establishing responsible governments. This marks the move toward self-governance and independence from direct British rule.

1898

Constitution Referendum

Constitution referendum is held. Australians vote on the draft Constitution for federation, deciding whether to unite as one nation.

1901

1 January - Federation & Constitution

The Constitution comes into effect on 1 January. Six separate British colonies officially unite into the Commonwealth of Australia. This is the birth of modern Australia as a nation.

1902

Women Gain Right to Vote

Women gain the right to vote in federal elections. Australia becomes one of the first nations in the world to grant women voting rights - a major progressive milestone.

1967

Aboriginal Rights Referendum

A referendum is held. Over 90% of Australians vote Yes to count Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples in the Census, removing federal discrimination and extending equal rights.

Essential Timeline Summary

  • 1788: First Fleet arrives (26 January) - Australia Day origin
  • 1851: Gold discovered in NSW and Victoria
  • 1898: Constitution referendum year
  • 1901: Federation (1 January) - Constitution takes effect
  • 1902: Women granted right to vote
  • 1967: Aboriginal peoples counted in Census (90%+ Yes vote)

Australian History Periods

Early Settlement (1788-1850s)

British colony established with convict transportation. Gradual expansion of settlement. Indigenous Australian population decimated by disease and conflict.

Gold Rush Era (1851+)

Massive population growth following gold discoveries. Development of new regions. Increasing ethnic diversity among immigrants.

Self-Government (1860s-1890s)

Colonies gain control over their own affairs. Responsible government established in each colony. Road to federation begins.

Federation Period (1898-1901)

Constitutional debate and negotiation. Referendum on union held (1898). Creation of the Commonwealth of Australia (1901).

Early Commonwealth (1901-1967)

Nation building and institutional development. Gradual expansion of women's rights. Continuous debates about Aboriginal rights.

Modern Australia (1967-Present)

Aboriginal rights recognition milestone (1967). Evolution toward multicultural society. Democratic development and constitutional evolution.

📊 Important Numbers & Facts

States & Territories

6 States: New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia, Western Australia, Tasmania

2 Territories: Northern Territory, Australian Capital Territory

Total: 8 jurisdictions make up the Commonwealth of Australia

Federal Parliament - Senate

76 Senators total

12 Senators per State (6 states × 12 = 72)

2 Senators per Territory (2 territories × 2 = 4)

Senate: Upper House | Reviews and debates laws

Federal Parliament - House of Representatives

151 Members of the House of Representatives

House: Lower House | Introduces most legislation

Representation: Based on population of each state/territory

Federal Parliament Total: 76 Senators + 151 Members = 227

Executive Government

1 Monarch: King or Queen of Australia

1 Governor-General: Represents the Monarch in Australia

1 Prime Minister: Head of Government; leads majority party

Cabinet Ministers: Leads government departments and agencies

Judicial System

1 High Court: Supreme court of Australia; final authority

Federal Courts: Handle federal law cases

State/Territory Courts: Handle local laws and matters

District Courts, Magistrates Courts: Lower-level courts

Voting & Elections

Eligible voters: Australian citizens, 18+ years old

Compulsory voting: Required to vote in federal elections

Senate elections: Every 6 years (staggered)

House elections: Every 3 years (maximum term)

Constitution & Law-Making

Constitution: Written in 1898-1901; Australia's highest law

Powers: Federal government has specific constitutional powers

Amendments: Require referendum (vote of Australian people)

Parliament: Makes laws within constitutional limits

👔 Australian Government Positions

👑 Head of State: The Monarch

Title: King or Queen of Australia

Current: King Charles III

Role: Constitutional head of state; does not govern day-to-day

Powers: Symbolic authority; acts on advice of government ministers

Represented in Australia by: Governor-General

🏛️ Governor-General

Title: Governor-General of Australia

Role: Represents the King in Australia; head of state ceremonially

Appointed by: The King on advice of the Prime Minister

Powers: Formal constitutional powers (signs laws, dissolves Parliament); mostly ceremonial

Term: Usually 5 years

Current: General David Hurley

🔴 Prime Minister

Title: Prime Minister of Australia

Role: Head of Government; chief executive; leads the nation

Selection: Member of Parliament from majority party; chosen by that party

Powers: Leads Cabinet, sets government policy, chief spokesperson

Requirements: Must be Member of Parliament (House or Senate)

Report to: Parliament; answerable to the people

📋 Cabinet Ministers

Count: Typically 20-30 Cabinet ministers

Selection: Chosen by Prime Minister from Parliament members

Roles: Each leads a government department/ministry

Examples:

  • Minister for Education
  • Minister for Health
  • Minister for Defence
  • Minister for Foreign Affairs
  • Treasurer (manages economy)
  • Attorney-General (chief law officer)

Responsibility: Implement government policies; manage departments

⚖️ Chief Justice

Title: Chief Justice of the High Court

Role: Head of the judicial system; leads the High Court

Court: Presides over the High Court of Australia (7 justices)

Powers: Interprets Constitution; final authority on legal matters

Independence: Completely independent from Executive and Parliament

Appointed by: Governor-General on advice of government (but strictly impartial)

🏠 Speaker of the House

Role: Presides over House of Representatives debates

Elected by: Members of House of Representatives

Responsibilities: Maintains order; ensures rules followed; impartial chair

Powers: Controls debate, recognizes speakers, enforces parliamentary procedure

📍 President of the Senate

Role: Presides over Senate debates (similar to Speaker in House)

Elected by: Senators

Title: President of the Senate (not a political party position)

Responsibilities: Maintains order; ensures rules followed

👥 Parliamentary Members

House of Representatives (Lower House): 151 Members

  • Elected by voters from their local electorate
  • Serve 3-year terms (maximum)
  • Must be citizens, 18+ years old, resident 3+ years

Senate (Upper House): 76 Senators

  • Elected by voters (proportional representation)
  • Serve 6-year terms (staggered; half elected every 3 years)
  • Same eligibility as House members

Government Positions Hierarchy

  • 👑 King/Queen - Constitutional Monarch (symbolic head)
  • 🏛️ Governor-General - Represents Monarch in Australia
  • 🔴 Prime Minister - Head of Government; chief executive
  • 📋 Cabinet Ministers - 20-30 ministers leading departments
  • 📍 Speaker/President - Chairs House/Senate debates
  • 👥 MPs/Senators - 227 members total (151 House + 76 Senate)
  • ⚖️ Chief Justice - Head of judicial system; interprets Constitution
